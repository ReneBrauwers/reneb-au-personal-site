My foundation is hands-on: software, enterprise integration, automation and cloud. It lets me move from executive consequence to engineering reality without losing either. That grounding matters when a clean strategy meets legacy constraints, delivery pressure and the consequences of operating change.

04

Operating position

Architecture earns its place by improving the decision.

My role is not to create more architecture. It is to make the consequences of a choice clear enough for people to act.

01

Start with the outcome

Lead with the business consequence and the decision that needs to be made. Technology comes after context.

02

Make trade-offs explicit

Good advice makes cost, risk, reversibility and organisational impact understandable—not artificially simple.

03

Stay close to delivery

Strategy loses value when it cannot survive engineering reality. Keep the feedback loop between intent and execution short.

Protect the outcome, not the architecture function.

— An operating principle
